Quick gear hacks: turn household items into set saviors

Simple, budget-friendly tricks to repurpose common items for indie film production.

Takeaway

Every cheap household object can become a reliable piece of film gear.

What happened

While shooting a short in a cramped apartment, I ran out of light diffusion material. I grabbed a white shower curtain and a few cereal boxes, rigging them to soften harsh light. The result was a surprisingly even, cinematic look without spending a dime.

Why it matters

Indie budgets are tight, and gear costs can stall projects. Finding low-cost alternatives keeps momentum and encourages creative problem‑solving. Plus, it reduces reliance on rented equipment that may be unavailable.

How to think about it

Assess the visual problem first, then scan your surroundings for objects with similar optical properties. Test the makeshift solution on a small scale before committing. Document what works for future shoots.

Pros
  • Cost‑free
  • Readily available
  • Encourages creativity
Cons
  • May lack durability
  • Variable quality
  • Requires extra setup time
Watch out

Ensure improvised gear doesn't pose safety hazards or cause fire risks.

FAQ

Can a shower curtain replace a professional diffuser?

It softens harsh light well enough for tight shots, though it may not be as uniform as a dedicated diffuser.

What household item works as a cheap reflector?

A white poster board or a piece of aluminum foil on cardboard creates an effective bounce surface.

How to stabilize makeshift rigs?

Use clamps, sandbags, or even heavy books to keep improvised gear steady during takes.
