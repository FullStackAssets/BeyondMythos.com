The Switch Luber's Handbook

Lube your way to smoother typing

Takeaway

Proper switch lubrication can greatly improve your typing experience

What happened

Recently, we've seen a surge in switch lubrication methods and products, leaving many enthusiasts wondering which approach is best. From thick, heavy greases to thin, synthetic oils, the options can be overwhelming. Our team put several popular methods to the test to find out what really works.

Why it matters

A well-lubricated switch can mean the difference between a smooth, consistent typing experience and a noisy, unreliable one. Proper lubrication can also extend the lifespan of your switches, saving you money in the long run. With so many options available, it's essential to choose the right method for your needs.

How to think about it

When selecting a switch lubricant, consider the type of switch you're using, as well as your personal typing style. For example, linear switches may benefit from a lighter lubricant, while tactile switches may require something thicker. Always clean your switches before applying lubricant, and start with a small amount to avoid over-lubrication.

Pros
  • Smoother typing
  • Increased switch lifespan
  • Reduced noise
Cons
  • Messy application
  • Over-lubrication risks
  • Cost
Watch out

Be careful not to over-lubricate, as this can attract dust and debris, negating the benefits of lubrication

FAQ

What's the best switch lubricant?

The best lubricant for you will depend on your switch type and personal preference, but popular options include Krytox and Tribosys

How often should I lube my switches?

The frequency of lubrication will depend on your typing habits and environment, but a good rule of thumb is to re-lube every 6-12 months

Can I use any type of oil to lube my switches?

No, not all oils are suitable for switch lubrication - avoid using cooking oils or other household oils, as they can damage your switches or attract dust
