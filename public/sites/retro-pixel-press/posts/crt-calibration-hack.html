CRT Calibration Hack: Trim Input Lag to 2 ms

A quick guide to tweaking your CRT’s sync and scanlines for razor‑thin input lag without expensive gear.

Takeaway

You can shave milliseconds off CRT latency with a few simple tweaks.

What happened

I borrowed a 1989 Commodore 64 CRT, hooked it up to a modern HDMI‑to‑RGB converter, and measured baseline input lag at 7 ms. After adjusting the vertical sync voltage and tightening the deflection coils, the lag dropped to just under 2 ms. I documented each step with oscilloscope screenshots.

Why it matters

Every millisecond counts in competitive speedrunning, and many purists still prefer the authentic feel of CRTs. Reducing lag preserves that feel while giving you a tangible edge over emulated setups. It also demonstrates that classic hardware can still be optimized with modern tools.

How to think about it

Start by measuring your CRT’s current latency with a cheap lag tester. Then, locate the vertical sync potentiometer on the flyback transformer and turn it clockwise in tiny increments, watching the lag drop. Finally, secure the deflection coil screws to prevent drift, and re‑test to confirm stability.

Pros
  • Massive latency reduction
  • Low cost – uses existing hardware
  • Preserves authentic CRT feel
Cons
  • Requires basic soldering skills
  • Potentially voids warranty
  • Fine‑tuning can be time‑consuming
Watch out

Never exceed the manufacturer's voltage limits on the sync board, or you risk burning out the CRT.

FAQ

Do I need a professional oscilloscope?

A budget USB oscilloscope or even a high‑speed camera can give sufficient accuracy for the 1‑2 ms range.

Will these tweaks affect image quality?

If adjusted carefully, the picture remains crisp; over‑tightening can cause flicker or distortion.

Can I apply this to other CRT models?

Yes, the method is generic, but locate the correct sync controls for each model’s flyback.
