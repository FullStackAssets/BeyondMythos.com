Pixel Boost: Rewiring SNES Power Supply for Stable Voltage

A quick guide to rewiring your SNES power supply for rock‑steady voltage during marathon runs.

Takeaway

A stable power supply prevents resets and keeps your run smooth.

What happened

We opened a standard SNES AC adapter, replaced the aging electrolytic capacitors with modern low‑ESR parts, and added a simple voltage regulator. The console powered up without the usual voltage sag during intensive gameplay.

Why it matters

Power fluctuations are a silent killer for speedrunners, causing sudden resets that waste minutes. A reliable supply ensures the console stays on target from start to finish, protecting both your record and hardware.

How to think about it

When troubleshooting a run, check the adapter voltage under load, and consider upgrading if you see droop. Simple mods like a regulator can be done with basic soldering tools and a multimeter, giving you a clear view of the power health before each attempt.

Pros
  • Longer console lifespan
  • Fewer unexpected resets
  • Minimal cost
Cons
  • Requires soldering skill
  • Opening adapters voids warranty
  • Needs proper component selection
Watch out

Make sure the regulator’s current rating matches the SNES’s draw to avoid overheating.

FAQ

Do I need a specific type of regulator?

A 5V linear regulator with enough headroom for the SNES’s ~1.5A draw works well.

Can I use a pre‑built power supply instead of modifying?

Yes, a modern 5V/2A wall wart can replace the original adapter without any internal changes.

Is this mod safe for the original circuitry?

If you follow proper polarity and use quality components, the mod is safe and often improves overall stability.
