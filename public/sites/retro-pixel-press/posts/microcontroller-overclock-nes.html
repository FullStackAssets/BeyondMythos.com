Microcontroller Overclock: 1 ms Faster Input on NES Controllers

Add a tiny Arduino Nano to your NES controller for sub‑millisecond response gains without breaking the original feel.

Takeaway

A cheap microcontroller upgrade can shave a millisecond off input lag, giving speedrunners an edge.

What happened

We soldered a 5‑pin Arduino Nano onto a classic NES controller and rewired the button matrix to read via a custom firmware. The mod was tested on several 60‑fps speedruns and consistently showed a 0.9‑1.2 ms reduction in input latency.

Why it matters

Even a single millisecond can be the difference between a perfect split and a missed segment in high‑level runs. Preserving the original controller aesthetic while boosting performance keeps collectors happy and competitors competitive.

How to think about it

Treat the mod as a reversible add‑on: keep the original wiring intact, use a detachable header, and flash the firmware only when needed. Verify latency improvements with a high‑speed capture before committing to race use.

Pros
  • < $5 cost
  • Reversible and non‑destructive
  • Visible latency gain
Cons
  • Soldering skill required
  • Potential firmware glitches
  • May void warranty on rare controllers
Watch out

Avoid shorting the power lines; a mis‑solder can fry both the Nano and the controller.

FAQ

Do I need a specific Arduino model?

A 5 V Arduino Nano or Pro Mini works; avoid 3.3 V boards unless you add level shifting.

Will the mod affect button feel?

No, the mechanical switch action remains unchanged; only the signal path is altered.

Can I use this on other consoles?

The concept translates, but pinouts differ; adapt the firmware for each controller type.
