DIY CRT Lag Reducer: Cut Input Lag on Your SNES

A quick mod that slashes CRT lag without breaking your console.

Takeaway

A few cheap parts can shave precious milliseconds off your SNES runs.

What happened

We tested a simple capacitor‑based lag reducer on a classic SNES hooked to a CRT. After the tweak, input‑to‑display latency dropped by roughly 10 ms, a noticeable gain for tight speedruns. The mod was installed in under an hour with basic soldering tools.

Why it matters

Even tiny latency improvements can be the difference between a PB and a missed split in competitive runs. For collectors, the mod preserves the authentic feel of CRT play while modernizing performance. It also demonstrates that low‑cost hardware tweaks remain viable in a world of software emulation.

How to think about it

Start by measuring your baseline lag with a light‑sensor rig, then identify the CRT's sync board where a 0.1 µF electrolytic capacitor can be added in parallel to the video input line. Use a non‑polarized capacitor to avoid polarity issues, and keep solder joints short to prevent signal degradation. After installation, re‑measure to confirm the improvement and fine‑tune if needed.

Pros
  • Cost under $5
  • No firmware changes required
  • Reversible with minimal risk
Cons
  • Requires basic soldering skill
  • May void warranty on rare consoles
  • Effect varies by CRT model
Watch out

Never use polarized electrolytic capacitors in the video path, as they can damage the CRT circuitry.

FAQ

Will this mod affect video quality?

No, the capacitor only smooths the sync signal and does not alter picture sharpness.

Can I use a ceramic capacitor instead?

Yes, a 0.1 µF ceramic works equally well and is more temperature‑stable.

Do I need to open the CRT itself?

No, the mod is applied to the SNES's video output board, keeping the CRT untouched.
