Pixel-Boost: Rewiring Your SNES Power Supply for Consistent Frame Timing

Swap the SNES PSU for a regulated module and shave off frame‑time jitter in minutes.

Takeaway

A stable power supply can tighten timing and improve repeatability for speedruns.

What happened

While testing a classic SNES run, I noticed occasional frame‑time spikes that threw off my inputs. I replaced the original brick with a modern 9V regulated supply and logged the timing differences. The results showed a noticeable drop in jitter, making the runs smoother.

Why it matters

Power consistency directly influences the console's internal clock, which speedrunners rely on for precise input windows. Even microsecond variations can be the difference between a PB and a miss. Upgrading the PSU is a low‑cost, high‑impact tweak for anyone chasing perfection.

How to think about it

Treat your power source like any other part of the controller chain—measure, compare, and iterate. Start with a cheap, well‑reviewed regulator, test with a frame‑counter, then decide if further filtering is needed. The goal is a repeatable baseline, not just a one‑off boost.

Pros
  • Immediate jitter reduction
  • Cheap and reversible
  • Improves overall console stability
Cons
  • Requires opening the console
  • Potential warranty void
  • Needs a reliable regulator
Watch out

Make sure the replacement supply matches the SNES's voltage and polarity to avoid hardware damage.

FAQ

Do I need a soldering iron for the swap?

A simple plug‑in regulator works for most models; soldering is only needed for hard‑wired mods.

Will this affect the console's audio output?

No, a clean supply actually reduces audio noise caused by voltage ripple.

Can I use any 9V regulator?

Choose one with low dropout and at least 1A rating; cheap linear regulators are fine for the SNES.
