Unlocking FM Audio on the Master System: A Simple Mod

Add FM sound to your Sega Master System with a cheap mod and hear classics like Alex Kidd in richer detail.

Takeaway

A low-cost FM chip upgrade brings new life to vintage Master System games.

What happened

We soldered a small FM audio board onto the console’s sound port and tested it with "Sonic the Hedgehog" on a PAL cartridge. The result was a crisp, melodic soundtrack that the original hardware never produced.

Why it matters

FM audio expands the auditory palette of a beloved platform, making preservation efforts more authentic and giving speedrunners clearer audio cues. It also showcases how simple hardware tweaks can modernize legacy consoles.

How to think about it

When evaluating a mod, weigh the impact on gameplay experience against the effort and risk. Start with a reversible prototype, document each step, and test with multiple games to ensure compatibility.

Pros
  • Cheap (<$15)
  • Visible audio improvement
  • Reversible install
Cons
  • Soldering required
  • Potential warranty void
  • Limited to PAL region games
Watch out

Avoid shorting the sound port; double‑check polarity before powering the console.

FAQ

Do I need a special screwdriver?

A small Phillips #0 works for the case screws; the mod itself uses a soldering iron.

Will this affect my console’s video output?

No, the FM board only taps the audio line, leaving video untouched.

Can I use the mod on a NTSC Master System?

The FM chip is region‑agnostic, but you’ll need a compatible game ROM that supports FM sound.
