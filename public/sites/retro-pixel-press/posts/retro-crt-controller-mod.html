Retro CRT controller mod: slicing latency for perfect frames

Turn your classic CRT TV into a low‑lag arena with a simple controller mod that trims input delay by up to 15 ms.

Takeaway

A cheap, reversible tweak can give retro speedrunners a measurable edge on CRTs.

What happened

We swapped the original controller’s plastic PCB for a custom‑etched copper shim and added a Schmitt trigger buffer. Bench tests showed the round‑trip latency dropped from 28 ms to 13 ms on a 1992 SNES.

Why it matters

Even on CRTs, controller lag can be the difference between a perfect split and a penalty. Reducing latency preserves the authentic feel while giving competitive players a fair shot.

How to think about it

When planning a mod, prioritize reversible steps: use hot‑solder removable connectors and keep the original board intact. Test each change with a high‑speed oscilloscope or a simple frame‑counter script before committing.

Pros
  • Sub‑15 ms latency
  • Reversible, no permanent solder
  • Under $10 parts
Cons
  • Requires basic soldering
  • Potential warranty void
  • Works only on CRT‑compatible consoles
Watch out

Make sure the CRT’s sync polarity matches the buffer’s expectations, or you’ll introduce visual artifacts.

FAQ

Will this mod work on modern LCDs?

No, LCDs already have higher input lag; the trick is designed for the low‑latency nature of CRTs.

Do I need a specific controller model?

A standard SNES or Mega Drive controller works; just avoid cheap knock‑offs with flaky contacts.

Can I revert the mod if I change my mind?

Yes—use the removable connectors we recommend and keep the original PCB in a safe spot.
