Reviving a stagnant starter: tips and tricks

Breathe new life into a flagging starter

Takeaway

Regular feeding and patience can revive a stalled sourdough starter

What happened

Many home sourdough bakers have experienced the frustration of a starter that's lost its mojo, refusing to bubble or rise. This can happen due to neglect, poor storage, or simply the natural cycle of a starter's life. By understanding the causes, bakers can take proactive steps to prevent stagnation and revive their starters when needed.

Why it matters

A healthy starter is the backbone of sourdough baking, responsible for the bread's signature flavor and texture. When a starter falters, the entire baking process is affected, leading to disappointing loaves. By mastering starter maintenance, bakers can ensure consistency and quality in their bread, even when faced with setbacks.

How to think about it

To revive a stagnant starter, bakers should first assess the environment and feeding schedule, making adjustments as necessary. Regular feeding, monitoring of temperature and humidity, and gentle handling can help coax a starter back to health. It's also crucial to be patient, as reviving a starter can take time and repeated efforts.

Pros
  • Improved bread flavor
  • Increased baking consistency
  • Reduced waste
Cons
  • Time-consuming
  • Requires patience
  • May not always succeed
Watch out

Be cautious not to overfeed, as this can lead to an overactive starter that's difficult to manage

FAQ

How often should I feed my starter?

Feed your starter once a day, or more frequently if it's very active

Can I revive a starter that's been dormant for months?

Yes, but it may require more time and effort to regain its health and activity

What's the best way to store my starter when not in use?

Store your starter in the fridge, feeding it once a week to maintain its health
