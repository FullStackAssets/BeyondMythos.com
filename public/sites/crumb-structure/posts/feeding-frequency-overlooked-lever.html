Feeding Frequency: The Overlooked Lever

Adjusting how often you feed your starter can boost activity without changing flour or water ratios.

Takeaway

A consistent, slightly more frequent feed schedule often yields a stronger starter faster.

What happened

We tried feeding our starter every 8 hours instead of the usual 12, keeping the same 1:1:1 ratio, and watched it double its rise speed within two days.

Why it matters

A more active starter shortens bulk fermentation, improves crumb openness, and reduces the risk of sluggish rises, especially in cooler kitchens.

How to think about it

Start by noting your starter’s peak time, then shift feeding intervals by 2‑4 hours earlier, maintaining the same ratios; monitor volume and aroma, and adjust until peaks align with your schedule.

Pros
  • Faster rise times
  • More predictable activity
  • Better flavor development
Cons
  • Requires more frequent attention
  • Potential waste if over‑fed
  • May need temperature control
Watch out

Avoid feeding too often in warm environments, or the starter can become exhausted.

FAQ

Will feeding more often dilute the starter’s acidity?

No, as long as you keep the same flour‑water ratio, acidity stays balanced; it simply matures faster.

Can I use this schedule with whole‑grain flour?

Yes, just keep the same feeding ratio; whole‑grain may slow fermentation slightly, so watch the peaks.

What if I miss a feed?

A single missed feed is fine; just resume the new schedule and give the starter a bit more time to catch up.
