Restoring the IBM 5150: From Dust to Daylight

Step‑by‑step guide to bring an original IBM 5150 back to life, focusing on power, video, and keyboard.

Takeaway

A careful restoration can make a 30‑year‑old PC boot and display reliably again.

What happened

Owners frequently encounter dead power supplies and failing CGA cards after decades of storage. Replacing the PSU and cleaning the video board revives functionality.

Why it matters

The IBM 5150 is a cornerstone of PC history; restoring it preserves a tangible piece of computing evolution and offers a platform for vintage software demos.

How to think about it

Test the power supply with a multimeter, replace capacitors showing bulging, clean the video connector, and verify the keyboard matrix for stuck keys.

Pros
  • Authentic hardware experience
  • Educational value
  • Supports early PC software
Cons
  • Hard‑to‑find replacement parts
  • Limited performance by modern standards
  • Potential for electrical hazards
Watch out

Always unplug the machine before opening; capacitors can retain charge even after power is removed.

FAQ

Do I need a new power switch?

If the original switch feels loose or fails a continuity test, replace it with a compatible momentary switch.

Can I use a modern monitor with the CGA card?

Yes, a CGA‑to‑VGA adapter works for most monitors; ensure correct resolution settings.

Is the original 5 MHz clock reliable?

Clocks can drift; a crystal replacement restores accurate timing for software.
