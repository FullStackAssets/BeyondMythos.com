Elevate Your Espresso Extraction with a Simple Flow Rate Check

A quick trick to gauge your espresso flow and fine‑tune extraction without pricey tools.

Takeaway

Monitoring flow rate lets you spot under‑ or over‑extraction before it ruins a shot.

What happened

I noticed my espresso shots were inconsistent despite keeping dose and grind steady. By timing the first 30 ml of output, the variation became obvious. A simple stopwatch revealed a 2‑second spread between pulls.

Why it matters

Flow rate directly reflects how water interacts with the coffee puck, influencing balance, sweetness, and bitterness. Small timing differences can mean a shot that's sour or overly bitter, frustrating even seasoned home baristas.

How to think about it

Start a timer as soon as water contacts the puck, then stop at 30 ml (a kitchen spoon or graduated cup works). Aim for a consistent window—usually around 2–3 seconds for a standard dose. Adjust grind finer if it’s too fast, coarser if it lags.

Pros
  • No expensive equipment needed
  • Instant feedback on each pull
  • Helps dial in grind and dose quickly
Cons
  • Requires a reliable timer
  • Only a rough proxy for extraction
  • May need practice to read subtle changes
Watch out

Don’t rely solely on timing; taste and visual cues still matter.

FAQ

What if I don’t have a 30 ml measuring cup?

Use any small container and estimate the volume, or count roughly 2 spoons of liquid.

Is a kitchen timer accurate enough?

A basic stopwatch or phone timer is sufficient; the goal is consistency, not millisecond precision.

Can I use this method for pour‑over?

Yes, timing the first 30 ml of brew can also highlight flow issues in pour‑over.
