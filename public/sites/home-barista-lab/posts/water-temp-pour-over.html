Unlock the hidden power of water temperature for pour-over

A quick guide to dialing in the perfect brew temperature and why it matters more than you think.

Takeaway

Small temperature tweaks can dramatically improve extraction and flavor balance.

What happened

We brewed two identical pour‑over recipes, one at 92 °C and another at 96 °C, using the same beans and grind. The higher‑temp batch extracted faster and highlighted brighter acidity, while the cooler brew emphasized body. Taste tests confirmed noticeable differences.

Why it matters

Water temperature directly controls extraction rate, influencing sweetness, acidity, and bitterness. Home brewers often overlook it, relying on default kettle settings, which can mask a bean’s true character.

How to think about it

Start by measuring your kettle’s output with a thermometer, then aim for 93–95 °C for most single‑origin beans. Adjust ±2 °C based on roast level: cooler for dark roasts to avoid over‑extraction, hotter for light roasts to unlock nuance. Record your results and fine‑tune.

Pros
  • Better flavor clarity
  • Consistent extraction
  • Simple to implement
Cons
  • Requires thermometer
  • May need kettle upgrade
  • Adds a step to routine
Watch out

Avoid boiling water; a sudden temperature drop can throw off your target brew temperature.

FAQ

Do I need a fancy thermometer?

A basic digital kitchen thermometer works fine; accuracy within ±1 °C is enough.

What temperature for dark roasts?

Aim for the low end, around 90–92 °C, to prevent bitterness.

Can I trust the temperature reading on my electric kettle?

Most built‑in displays are approximate; calibrate with a separate thermometer for best results.
