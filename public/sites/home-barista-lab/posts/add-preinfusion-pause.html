Add a Pre‑Infusion Pause for Smoother Espresso

A quick pre‑infusion pause can smooth out channeling and improve flavor without new gear.

Takeaway

A brief pre‑infusion pause steadies water flow and yields a more balanced shot.

What happened

I set my machine to hold at low pressure for about two seconds before ramping to full pressure. The first shot tasted less harsh, and the crema held a richer amber hue. Repeating the pause a few times confirmed the effect was repeatable.

Why it matters

Channeling often creates uneven extraction, leading to sour or bitter notes. By giving the puck a moment to wet evenly, the water distributes more uniformly, unlocking hidden sweetness in the beans. This tiny tweak can make a noticeable difference without buying a new espresso machine.

How to think about it

If your espresso feels thin or the crema fades quickly, try a short pre‑infusion pause. Most modern machines let you program a low‑pressure soak; if yours doesn’t, manually start the brew, stop the pump after a second, wait, then resume. Keep the pause under three seconds to avoid over‑extraction.

Pros
  • Easier flavor balance
  • No extra equipment needed
  • Works on most home machines
Cons
  • Requires manual timing on older machines
  • May need trial‑and‑error for optimal length
  • Not a cure‑all for poor grind or tamp
Watch out

Avoid extending the pause beyond three seconds, or you’ll risk under‑extraction and a flat shot.

FAQ

Can I use a timer to control the pause?

Yes, a kitchen timer or smartphone stopwatch works fine for consistency.

Does pre‑infusion work with all bean types?

It generally benefits most beans, especially those with a finer grind and higher density.

Will this increase my brew time significantly?

Only by a couple of seconds per shot, which is negligible for daily brewing.
