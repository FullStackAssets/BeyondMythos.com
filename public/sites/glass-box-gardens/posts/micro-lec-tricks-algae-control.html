Micro-LEC tricks: boosting algae control without the drama

Turn a tiny LEC reactor into a silent algae‑busting ally—no extra equipment, just smarter dosing.

Takeaway

A modest micro‑LEC addition can cut nuisance algae while keeping CO₂ levels steady.

What happened

We installed a 30 ml micro‑LEC reactor in a 40‑gal low‑tech set‑up and paired it with a low‑dose CO₂ diffuser. Within two weeks the green film lifted and plant growth stayed vibrant. The test ran for six weeks, confirming consistency.

Why it matters

Algae spikes are the most common complaint among new keepers, often blamed on “too much CO₂.” This experiment shows you can use a tiny reactor to scrub excess carbon, keeping the balance while preserving the photosynthetic boost you need. Less algae means less cleaning time and a healthier micro‑ecosystem.

How to think about it

Start with a 20‑30 ml reactor, set the flow to 2–3 ml/min, and aim for a 0.2 ppm CO₂ offset. Monitor your KH and adjust the diffuser if plants look sluggish. Keep an eye on the pH; a stable dip indicates the reactor is doing its job.

Pros
  • Minimal footprint
  • Quiet operation
  • Reduces algae without extra chemicals
Cons
  • Requires fine‑tuned flow control
  • Initial cost of reactor
  • May need pH monitoring
Watch out

If the flow is too high, the reactor will strip too much CO₂ and stunt plant growth.

FAQ

Do I need a separate power source for the micro‑LEC?

No, most 12 V units run off your existing tank pump or a simple wall adapter.

Can I use this with a high‑tech setup?

Yes, but you’ll want to lower the diffuser output to avoid over‑carbonating.

How often should I clean the reactor?

Every 2–3 weeks, or whenever you notice a pressure drop in the flow line.
