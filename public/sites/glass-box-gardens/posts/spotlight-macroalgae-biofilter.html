Spotlight: Macroalgae as a natural biofilter

Exploring how macroalgae can clean water, boost growth, and add a dynamic look to your planted tank.

Takeaway

Macroalgae are a low‑maintenance, self‑cleaning ally for healthier, greener tanks.

What happened

Last month we introduced a small patch of Chaetomorpha gracilis into a 55‑gallon low‑tech tank. Within two weeks the water clarity improved and the plants showed brighter foliage.

Why it matters

Macroalgae absorb excess nutrients, reducing algae spikes and easing CO₂ demand. Their fast growth also provides a natural food source for herbivorous fish and shrimp.

How to think about it

Start with a 5‑inch piece anchored to a rock or mesh and keep moderate lighting (1–2 W/gal). Monitor nitrate levels and trim regularly to prevent overgrowth.

Pros
  • Natural nutrient scrub
  • Adds movement
  • Provides supplemental food
Cons
  • Can outcompete slow growers
  • Requires occasional trimming
  • May need extra lighting
Watch out

If left unchecked, macroalgae can dominate the display and shade your foreground plants.

FAQ

Can I use macroalgae in a high‑tech tank?

Yes, but keep lighting and nutrient levels balanced to avoid excessive growth.

What species are best for beginners?

Chaetomorpha and Gracilaria are hardy, fast‑growing choices.

Do I need to fertilize macroalgae?

They thrive on dissolved nutrients, so additional fertilization is usually unnecessary.
