Micro-joinery: mastering dovetail pins with a single chisel

Turn a single chisel into a dovetail pin‑making powerhouse in your cramped shop.

Takeaway

One well‑placed chisel can replace a whole set of specialty pins.

What happened

I tried cutting a series of 1/8" dovetail pins using only my bench chisel and a wooden mallet. After a few adjustments to angle and depth, the pins fit snugly into a test joint.

Why it matters

Small shops often lack space for a full pin set, yet precise pins are critical for strength and aesthetics. Demonstrating a minimalist technique keeps the bench clear and the budget low.

How to think about it

Set the chisel at a shallow angle, tap gently, and let the wood grain do the work. Incrementally deepen the cut, checking fit each time, and use a scrap block to stabilize the workpiece.

Pros
  • Minimal tool count
  • Low cost
  • Easy to learn
Cons
  • Slower than a pin set
  • Requires careful angle control
  • Limited to small pins
Watch out

Avoid over‑driving the chisel; it can split the workpiece.

FAQ

Can I use a beveled chisel?

A flat‑back chisel gives the cleanest edge, but a beveled one works if you keep the angle consistent.

What wood works best?

Straight‑grain hardwoods like maple or oak hold the pins tight; softwoods may crush.

Do I need a stop block?

A simple stop block ensures uniform depth across multiple pins.
