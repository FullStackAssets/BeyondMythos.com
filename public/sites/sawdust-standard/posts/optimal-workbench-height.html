Benchmarked: The Surprising Truth About Workbench Height

Get the most from your workspace

Takeaway

A well-set workbench height boosts productivity and reduces fatigue

What happened

We spent a weekend in the shop, testing various workbench heights with different tasks and tools. The results were eye-opening, with significant differences in comfort and efficiency. From planing to joinery, each task had a sweet spot.

Why it matters

The right workbench height can mean the difference between a enjoyable hobby and a frustrating, back-aching chore. With many hobby woodworkers working in small, multi-purpose shops, optimizing this key aspect of the workspace is crucial. It affects not just comfort, but also the quality of work produced.

How to think about it

When setting up your workbench, consider the primary tasks you'll be performing and adjust the height accordingly. For example, planing and heavy sanding benefit from a slightly lower bench, while precision work like joinery may require a higher surface. Experiment to find what works best for you and your tools.

Pros
  • Increased comfort
  • Improved precision
  • Boosted productivity
Cons
  • Space constraints
  • Tool adjustment needed
  • Initial experimentation required
Watch out

Be cautious not to set your workbench too high, as this can lead to shoulder strain and decreased control over your tools

FAQ

What is the ideal workbench height?

It varies by task and user, but a good starting point is between 33-36 inches for most adult hobby woodworkers

How do I adjust my existing workbench?

Consider using adjustable legs or adding/removing shims to achieve the perfect height for your needs

Will this improvement make a big difference?

Yes, even small adjustments can significantly impact your comfort and the quality of your work
