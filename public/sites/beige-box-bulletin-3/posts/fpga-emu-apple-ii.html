FPGA Emulation of the Apple II

FPGA boards now emulate Apple II hardware with cycle‑accurate fidelity.

Takeaway

FPGA offers a hardware‑level Apple II experience without the original chassis.

What happened

Developers have programmed open‑source cores to replicate the Apple II's 6502 CPU, video, and I/O on FPGA platforms. The result runs original software with minimal latency.

Why it matters

Hardware emulation preserves timing‑sensitive behavior that software emulators sometimes miss, crucial for games and demos.

How to think about it

Choose an FPGA board with sufficient I/O, load the core, and connect a vintage monitor via composite or VGA adapters. Keep original ROM images for authenticity.

Pros
  • Accurate timing
  • Compact setup
  • Preserves original software
Cons
  • Initial learning curve
  • Requires hardware purchase
  • Limited peripheral support
Watch out

Ensure the ROM images are legally obtained before flashing.

FAQ

Do I need a real Apple II keyboard?

You can use a USB‑compatible replica or map keys to a modern keyboard.

Can I expand memory?

Some cores support configurable RAM up to 64 KB.

Is the FPGA board hot‑plug safe?

Power down before connecting or disconnecting peripherals.
