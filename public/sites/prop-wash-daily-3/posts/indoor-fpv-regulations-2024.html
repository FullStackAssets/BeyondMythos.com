What the 2024 Indoor FPV Rules Mean for Your Build

New indoor flight guidelines tighten safety standards, affecting component choices and flight practices.

Takeaway

Complying with the latest indoor regulations helps you avoid fines and keeps the hobby safe.

What happened

A city council adopted a set of indoor FPV rules that require low‑noise propellers, visible identification tags, and a maximum flight time of 10 minutes per session. Drone clubs began updating their sign‑up forms.

Why it matters

Non‑compliance can lead to venue bans or liability issues, especially in public indoor spaces. Understanding the rules lets you plan builds that meet the criteria without sacrificing performance.

How to think about it

Select propellers under the noise threshold and add bright stickers with your registration number. Keep battery capacity modest to stay within the flight‑time limit, and carry a quick‑swap battery system to stay legal. Review the venue’s specific add‑ons before each session.

Pros
  • Improved safety
  • Clear guidelines
  • Encourages community standards
Cons
  • Limits high‑thrust builds
  • May require extra accessories
  • Adds paperwork
Watch out

Ignoring the noise limit can result in immediate shutdown of the event.

FAQ

Do the rules apply to private venues?

They mainly target public spaces, but many private venues adopt them voluntarily.

What propeller size is considered low‑noise?

Generally 4‑inch to 5‑inch slow‑pitch props stay below the threshold.

Can I get an exemption for competition use?

Exemptions are rare and require a formal request to the venue authority.
