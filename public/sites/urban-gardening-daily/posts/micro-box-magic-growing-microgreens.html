Micro-Box Magic: Growing Microgreens on a Windowsill

Turn a spare windowsill into a thriving microgreen kitchen in under a week.

Takeaway

A tiny, fast-growing harvest is possible with just a light, a tray, and a bit of patience.

What happened

We set up a DIY microgreen tray using recycled containers, organic seed mix, and a LED grow light on a balcony apartment. Within 48 hours the seeds germinated, and by day five the first leaves unfurled, ready for a fresh salad topping.

Why it matters

Microgreens pack a nutritional punch, perfect for space‑constrained city living, and they add vibrant flavor to meals without a garden plot. Growing them at home also cuts food miles and reduces waste.

How to think about it

Start with a shallow, reusable tray, a quality seed blend, and keep the medium consistently moist. Place the tray under bright indirect light or a 12‑hour LED schedule, and harvest when the first true leaves appear.

Pros
  • Super fast growth
  • Minimal space needed
  • Nutrient dense
Cons
  • Requires daily watering
  • Short shelf‑life after harvest
  • Light intensity must be managed
Watch out

Avoid over‑watering; soggy media leads to mold and stunted growth.

FAQ

Can I use any container?

A shallow, food‑safe tray or even a repurposed take‑out box works, as long as it drains.

How often should I water?

Mist the surface once or twice daily to keep the soil damp but not soaked.

When is the right time to harvest?

Harvest at 7‑14 days, when the first true leaves are 2‑3 cm tall.
