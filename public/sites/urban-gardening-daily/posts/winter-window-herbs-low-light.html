Winter window herbs: thriving in low light

Learn how to keep fresh herbs alive on a sunny windowsill even when daylight dwindles.

Takeaway

Even in winter, a bright window can sustain a mini herb garden with the right species and care.

What happened

We tested a trio of cold‑tolerant herbs—chives, parsley, and thyme—on a south‑facing apartment window over December. Each plant received minimal supplemental light and a simple watering routine. By month’s end, they were still lush and ready to harvest.

Why it matters

Fresh herbs cut food costs and boost flavor, but winter often forces gardeners to abandon them. Proving they can survive indoors keeps the garden year‑round and maintains the habit of cooking with home‑grown greens.

How to think about it

Pick herbs that tolerate lower light, place them where they catch the most sun, use shallow trays with good drainage, and water just enough to keep the soil moist but not soggy. A 2‑hour LED grow light boost can bridge the darkest weeks. Rotate pots weekly to even out light exposure.

Pros
  • Low cost setup
  • Year‑round flavor boost
  • Minimal space needed
Cons
  • Limited harvest volume
  • Requires consistent watering
  • May need supplemental light
Watch out

Avoid overwatering; cold windows can mask drying soil, leading to root rot.

FAQ

Can I grow basil in winter on a windowsill?

Basil prefers warmth and strong light, so it’s better to stick with chives, parsley, or thyme during the cold months.

How much light is enough for winter herbs?

Aim for 4–6 hours of direct sun or provide a 12‑hour LED grow light at 15‑20 watts per square foot.

Do I need fertilizer for indoor winter herbs?

A light feed of liquid fertilizer once a month keeps nutrients up without burning delicate roots.
