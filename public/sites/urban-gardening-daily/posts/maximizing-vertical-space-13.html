Sky's the Limit: Boosting Yield with Trellises

Grow up, not out, with these smart trellis ideas

Takeaway

Using trellises can increase your balcony garden's yield by up to 50% without expanding its footprint

What happened

As urban gardening continues to gain popularity, space remains a significant constraint for many apartment dwellers. Traditional gardening methods often fail to fully utilize the available space, leading to lower yields. However, by incorporating trellises into their balcony gardens, individuals can significantly increase their produce. This approach allows plants like peas, beans, and tomatoes to grow upwards, maximizing vertical space.

Why it matters

For apartment dwellers and balcony gardeners, maximizing space is crucial for a bountiful harvest. By adopting a vertical gardening approach, individuals can grow a wider variety of plants, including those that typically require more space. This not only enhances the aesthetic appeal of the garden but also increases food production, making urban gardening a more sustainable and rewarding experience.

How to think about it

When integrating trellises into your balcony garden, consider the type of plants you want to grow and their specific support needs. Choose trellises made from durable, weather-resistant materials and ensure they are securely anchored to prevent them from toppling over in windy conditions. Regular pruning and training of the plants will also be necessary to maintain their shape and promote healthy growth.

Pros
  • Increases yield without expanding footprint
  • Adds visual appeal to the garden
  • Supports a wide range of climbing plants
Cons
  • Requires regular pruning and training
  • Can be costly for high-quality trellises
  • May obstruct sunlight for other plants
Watch out

Be cautious not to overload your trellis with too many plants, as this can lead to structural damage and reduced plant health

FAQ

What types of plants are best suited for trellises?

Climbing plants like peas, beans, tomatoes, and cucumbers thrive on trellises

How tall can I make my trellis?

The height of your trellis will depend on the available space and the type of plants you're growing, but a general rule of thumb is to keep it below 6 feet to avoid obstructing sunlight

Can I use trellises for plants that don't climb?

While trellises are ideal for climbing plants, you can also use them to provide support for top-heavy plants or to create a unique decorative element in your garden
