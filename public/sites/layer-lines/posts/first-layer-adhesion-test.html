Beyond the Bed: mastering first‑layer adhesion with a simple test print

A quick test to diagnose and fix first‑layer adhesion issues without expensive sensors.

Takeaway

A single test print reveals the root cause of adhesion problems and how to correct them.

What happened

Yesterday I printed a 20 mm calibration cube and the first layer kept lifting at the corners despite a freshly leveled bed. I switched to a 10 mm adhesion‑test pattern, tweaked temperature and Z‑offset, and finally nailed a perfect first layer.

Why it matters

First‑layer adhesion is the foundation of any successful print; failures waste filament, time, and morale. Understanding the underlying cause lets you avoid trial‑and‑error and keep the printer humming.

How to think about it

Print the adhesion test, observe where the filament detaches, then adjust one variable at a time—bed temp, nozzle height, surface prep—until the entire perimeter sticks. Keep notes so you can replicate the sweet spot on future prints.

Pros
  • Instant visual feedback
  • Requires no extra hardware
  • Works on any printer
Cons
  • Adds an extra print step
  • May mask deeper mechanical issues
  • Relies on user observation
Watch out

Don’t crank the bed temperature too high; it can warp the build surface or damage the printer.

FAQ

What temperature should I start with?

Begin at 60 °C for PLA, 100 °C for PETG, and adjust in 5 °C increments.

How do I know if my Z‑offset is right?

The filament should be slightly flattened, not squished or hovering.

Can I use this test on flexible filaments?

Yes, but use a softer surface like a PEI sheet to prevent tearing.
