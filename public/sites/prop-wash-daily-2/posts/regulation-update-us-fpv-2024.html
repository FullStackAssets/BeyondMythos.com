What the 2024 US FPV Regulation Means for Builders

New FAA guidelines tighten altitude limits and require remote ID for all FPV aircraft.

Takeaway

Compliance now involves adding a lightweight remote ID module and respecting 200‑foot ceiling.

What happened

The FAA released an update clarifying that all FPV drones, regardless of weight, need remote identification. The altitude ceiling for hobbyists remains 200 feet unless in a controlled area.

Why it matters

Non‑compliance can lead to fines or confiscation, especially at public events. Adding remote ID early avoids retrofitting later. Knowing the limits helps plan safe flight zones.

How to think about it

Select a compact remote ID transmitter that fits on your frame, and test it before flights. Keep flight logs to prove altitude compliance if asked. Re‑evaluate your flying spots to stay within legal limits.

Pros
  • Future‑proofs your build
  • Avoids legal trouble
  • Standardizes identification
Cons
  • Adds a few grams
  • May need antenna placement
  • Potential signal interference
Watch out

Ensure the remote ID antenna is clear of metal to maintain signal integrity.

FAQ

Do I need a separate license?

A standard Part 107 waiver is still required for commercial use; hobbyists follow the basic registration.

Can I use a DIY remote ID?

DIY solutions are acceptable if they meet FCC specifications.

What happens if I exceed 200 ft?

You risk enforcement action; stay within the limit or seek a waiver.
