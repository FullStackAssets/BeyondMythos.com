Chew Toy 101: What Every New Puppy Owner Should Know

A quick guide to picking safe, durable chew toys that keep your puppy happy and your house intact.

Takeaway

The right chew toy protects teeth, satisfies instincts, and saves your shoes.

What happened

We sent three of the top‑selling puppy chew toys to a house of first‑time owners and logged bite marks, squeak longevity, and cleanup time over a week. The rubber Kong survived the onslaught, the plush squeaker fell apart by day three, and the nylon rope held up but left fur‑stained teeth.

Why it matters

Puppies chew to soothe teething pain and explore. A poor‑quality toy can become a choking hazard or a shredded mess, while a good one supports dental health and reduces destructive behavior.

How to think about it

Start with the puppy’s size and chewing intensity, then prioritize non‑toxic, dishwasher‑safe materials. Test durability by a gentle tug before giving it loose, and rotate toys to keep interest high.

Pros
  • Durable material
  • Sized for teething
  • Easy to clean
Cons
  • Can be chewed apart
  • May hide small parts
  • Not for aggressive chewers
Watch out

Never leave a toy that shows fraying or pieces missing unsupervised.

FAQ

Are plush toys safe for teething puppies?

Only if they’re reinforced and you supervise; most will shred quickly and pose a choking risk.

How often should I replace chew toys?

Replace any toy showing wear, loose stuffing, or sharp edges—usually every 1–2 months for heavy chewers.

Is a frozen washcloth a good alternative?

It’s cheap and soothing, but it can become a soggy mess and isn’t as durable as purpose‑made toys.
