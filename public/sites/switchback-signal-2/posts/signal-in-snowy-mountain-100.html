Tackling a Snowy 100k with the Switchback Signal

Snow and ice add a new layer of challenge; the Signal’s outsole adapts surprisingly well to slippery terrain.

Takeaway

Pair the Signal with aggressive crampons for reliable traction in wintry ultramarathons.

What happened

In a 100‑km winter race, runners equipped with the Signal and micro‑spikes completed the icy sections without major slips. The shoe stayed comfortable despite low temperatures.

Why it matters

Winter ultrarunning demands gear that can transition from snow to rock; the Signal’s flexible sole maintains grip without stiffening.

How to think about it

Attach micro‑spikes securely, keep laces tight, and monitor foot temperature; replace socks if they become damp.

Pros
  • Flexible sole
  • Good cold‑weather feel
  • Compatible with spikes
Cons
  • Less insulation
  • Grip drops on hard ice
  • Needs extra accessories
Watch out

The shoe’s low stack height can expose the foot to cold on prolonged snow exposure.

FAQ

Do I need a different shoe for deep snow?

A shoe with higher water resistance can be preferable for deep, wet snow.

How many spikes per foot?

One set of micro‑spikes per shoe is sufficient for most icy sections.

Can I wear the Signal without spikes?

Yes, but expect reduced traction on hard-packed ice.
