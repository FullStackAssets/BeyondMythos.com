Choosing the Right Filter: Metal vs Paper

Each filter type shapes body, clarity, and cleanup ease differently.

Takeaway

Match filter material to desired cup profile and workflow.

What happened

You switch from paper to a reusable metal filter and notice a fuller mouthfeel but slightly muted acidity. Understanding the trade‑offs helps you decide when to use each for specific beans or taste goals.

Why it matters

Paper traps fine oils, yielding a cleaner cup; metal allows oils through, adding body. The choice influences perceived strength, clarity, and even health considerations like paper residue.

How to think about it

Use metal for bold, chocolatey beans where body shines, and paper for bright, floral coffees where clarity matters. Keep metal filters well‑rinsed to avoid oil buildup.

Pros
  • Metal: reusable, richer body
  • Paper: clean cup, easy disposal
  • Both: simple to swap
Cons
  • Metal: can retain oils
  • Paper: adds waste
  • Both: may affect brew time
Watch out

Never reuse a paper filter; it disintegrates and can release fibers into the brew.

FAQ

Does filter thickness matter?

Thicker metal meshes slow flow, increasing extraction; thin meshes speed it up.

Can I compost used paper filters?

Yes, most are biodegradable and safe for compost.

Do metal filters need seasoning?

Rinsing with hot water before first use removes manufacturing residues.
