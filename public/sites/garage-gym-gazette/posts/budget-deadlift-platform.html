Budget deadlift platform: build sturdy steel in a weekend

Turn cheap 2×4s and pipe into a rock‑solid deadlift platform without breaking the bank.

Takeaway

You can get a safe, level platform for under $200 using common garage materials.

What happened

Last weekend I salvaged 2×4s from a pallet and welded a 4‑by‑8 foot steel frame. After bolting down plywood and topping with rubber, the platform held 600 lb deadlifts without flex.

Why it matters

A stable platform protects your plates, improves lift confidence, and prevents floor damage—critical for anyone squatting or deadlifting in a garage.

How to think about it

Measure your rack footprint, use 2×4 joists spaced 16″, secure with pipe brackets, then add a plywood sheet and interlocking rubber mats for grip.

Pros
  • Cheap under $200
  • Fast weekend build
  • Durable for heavy lifts
Cons
  • Requires basic welding
  • Limited aesthetic polish
  • Needs periodic tightening
Watch out

Check that the bolts are torqued after the first few sessions to avoid loosening.

FAQ

Can I use standard 2×4s or do they need to be pressure‑treated?

Standard kiln‑dried 2×4s are fine as long as they’re dry and free of rot.

Do I need a concrete slab underneath?

A solid concrete slab is ideal, but a level garage floor with plywood underlayment works.

What rubber thickness is best?

A ½‑inch interlocking mat offers enough cushioning without sacrificing stability.
