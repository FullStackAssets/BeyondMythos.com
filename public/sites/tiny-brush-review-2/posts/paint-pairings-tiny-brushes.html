Paint Pairings: Which Paints Flow Best with Tiny Brushes

Choosing the right paint consistency for miniature detail work.

Takeaway

Smooth, low‑vis paints keep tiny brush tips from splaying.

What happened

We compared three popular acrylic lines on a 28mm elf archer, using a 0.5 mm brush for eye and blade highlights. The low‑vis formula delivered crisp lines, while the thicker paint caused bristle fanning.

Why it matters

A brush that sags or spreads muddies fine details, forcing repainting and slowing progress.

How to think about it

Select paints labeled “fine detail” or thin them with a drop of water or medium. Test the flow on a palette before applying to the model.

Pros
  • Sharper lines
  • Less brush strain
  • Easier cleanup
Cons
  • May require more coats
  • Can be less opaque
  • Thin paints dry faster
Watch out

Avoid over‑thinning; a paint that’s too runny will lose coverage on small areas.

FAQ

Do washes work with thin paints?

They can, but keep the wash light to prevent tip swelling.

Is a medium better than water?

A dedicated medium maintains pigment load while adjusting flow.

Can I use enamel paints?

Enamels are generally too thick for tiny brushes without significant thinning.
