Feeding Your Starter: Timing Tricks for Beginners

Learn when and how often to feed your sourdough starter to keep it lively without the guesswork.

Takeaway

Consistent, well‑timed feedings give a stronger, more predictable starter.

What happened

A new batch of starter was left on the counter for days without feeding, turning sluggish and developing off‑flavors. After resetting the feeding schedule, the starter revived quickly and produced a crisp crumb in the next loaf.

Why it matters

A starter that’s fed on a reliable rhythm behaves predictably during bulk fermentation, reducing surprise failures and saving time. Beginners often think “more is better,” but over‑feeding can dilute activity and waste flour.

How to think about it

Treat feeding like a calendar: count the hours since the last feed and match it to room temperature—roughly 12 h in a warm kitchen, 24 h in a cooler spot. Use the same flour type each time to keep the microbial community stable.

Pros
  • Stronger rise
  • Consistent flavor
  • Less waste
Cons
  • Requires routine
  • May need temperature monitoring
  • Can be confusing at first
Watch out

Don’t let the starter sit for more than a day without a feed in a warm kitchen, or it can become overly acidic.

FAQ

How often should I feed a starter kept at room temperature?

In a typical 20‑22 °C kitchen, a 12‑hour interval works well; adjust longer in cooler spaces.

Can I use whole‑grain flour for feeding?

Yes, but whole‑grain introduces more nutrients that can speed fermentation; you may need to shorten the interval.

What’s the sign that a starter is under‑fed?

A thin, watery layer of liquid (hooch) on top and a sour smell indicate it needs a fresh feed.
