First week feeding: balancing nutrition and curiosity

Your puppy’s first week of meals can set the tone for lifelong health—here’s how to get it right.

Takeaway

A structured, varied feeding plan prevents digestive upsets and builds good habits early.

What happened

I swapped my new lab puppy’s ad‑hoc kibble schedule for a measured three‑times‑daily routine, mixing wet food with a small protein treat. By day five, his energy steadied and stools normalized.

Why it matters

Puppies’ guts are still developing; erratic feeding can cause gas, diarrhea, and a negative association with meals. Consistency also teaches them when to expect food, reducing anxiety.

How to think about it

Aim for 3–4 small meals, each 10% of daily caloric needs, and rotate protein sources weekly. Use a timer, measure portions, and keep a simple log to spot patterns.

Pros
  • Steady energy levels
  • Easier house‑training
  • Clear health baseline
Cons
  • More prep time
  • Need to track calories
  • Potential waste if portions mis‑estimated
Watch out

Avoid sudden diet changes after the first week, as they can trigger digestive flare‑upss.

FAQ

How many calories should a 8‑week puppy get?

Roughly 55 kcal per pound of expected adult weight, divided into 3–4 meals.

Can I add human food?

Small, dog‑safe bites like boiled chicken are fine, but keep them <10% of total intake.

When should I switch to adult food?

Usually around 12 months, but follow your vet’s guidance based on breed and size.
