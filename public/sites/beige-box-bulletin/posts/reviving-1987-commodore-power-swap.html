Reviving the 1987 Commodore: A DIY Power Supply Swap

Swap the original power supply for a modern, reliable unit and bring your 1987 Commodore back to life.

Takeaway

A proper power supply upgrade can save your retro machine from frequent crashes and extend its lifespan.

What happened

I discovered the Commodore's original PSU was flaky, causing random boot hangs and occasional smoke. After locating a compatible modern 12 V/5 A unit and rewiring with proper connectors, the machine boots cleanly and runs stable.

Why it matters

Power reliability is the backbone of any restoration; a bad supply masks other issues and can damage delicate components. Upgrading to a stable source lets you focus on genuine hardware quirks rather than power noise, preserving both the machine and your sanity.

How to think about it

Identify the exact voltage and current specs of the original PSU, then find a modern replacement that matches or exceeds them while keeping the same polarity. Use a bench power supply for testing before permanent wiring, and label all connections to avoid mistakes. Document the swap so future tinkers know the new configuration.

Pros
  • Stable voltage
  • Reduced heat
  • Longer component life
Cons
  • Initial cost
  • Need for custom wiring
  • Potential loss of originality
Watch out

Double‑check polarity and connector pin‑out before powering up to avoid frying the motherboard.

FAQ

Can I use a generic ATX PSU for a Commodore?

Only if you step down the voltage with a regulator; a direct ATX 5 V/12 V rail will likely over‑voltage the board.

Do I need to keep the original PSU for authenticity?

For display purposes you can keep it as a non‑functional artifact, but the working machine should use the modern unit.

Will this affect the machine's original sound or timing?

No, a clean supply actually improves audio fidelity and timing stability compared to a failing original.
