Reviving the IBM 5150: A Fresh Power Supply Swap

Swapping the power supply in an IBM 5150 can bring your original PC back to life with minimal hassle.

Takeaway

A proper power supply replacement restores stability without hunting for scarce OEM parts.

What happened

We opened a decade‑old IBM 5150, found the original PSU dead, and fitted a modern ATX unit with a custom voltage regulator. After wiring and a brief test, the machine powered up cleanly and ran a diagnostic cartridge.

Why it matters

Power‑related failures are the most common cause of dead retro rigs, and sourcing original supplies is near impossible. A reliable modern substitute keeps the hardware usable and extends its lifespan for enthusiasts and educators alike.

How to think about it

First verify the original PSU’s voltage specs, then select an ATX unit that can be stepped down safely. Use a DC‑DC regulator or a simple resistor ladder to match the 5 V and 12 V rails, and test each line before connecting to the motherboard. Keep wiring tidy and label adapters for future maintenance.

Pros
  • Low cost
  • Improved reliability
  • Preserves original chassis
Cons
  • May void originality
  • Requires custom wiring
  • Potential heat increase
Watch out

Double‑check polarity and connector pin‑out before you solder.

FAQ

Can I use a standard ATX PSU?

Yes, with a simple voltage regulator and proper adapters you can safely power a 5150.

Do I need to modify the case?

Only minimal drilling for the new connector; keep the original mounting holes intact.

Will this affect the machine’s value?

Collectors prefer original parts, but a functional system often outweighs a static display for most hobbyists.
