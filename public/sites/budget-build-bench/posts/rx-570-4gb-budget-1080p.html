RX 570 4GB: Still a viable 1080p workhorse for under $100

The Radeon RX 570 proves you can game 1080p on a shoestring without breaking the bank.

Takeaway

A used RX 570 can deliver solid 1080p performance for under $100, keeping your build affordable.

What happened

Recent market fluctuations have pushed many older AMD cards into the second‑hand arena at surprisingly low prices. The RX 570, once a mid‑range staple, now often sells for under $100 on reputable platforms. This creates an opening for budget builders seeking decent performance without new‑card premiums.

Why it matters

For builders constrained by tight budgets, every dollar counts toward a balanced system. An affordable GPU that still handles modern titles at 1080p means you can allocate more to CPU, storage, or a better PSU. It also extends the life of older components, reducing electronic waste.

How to think about it

When hunting for a used RX 570, verify the card’s physical condition, ask about its usage history, and request a short stress test video if possible. Pair it with a modest CPU to avoid bottlenecks, and consider a modest overclock if temperatures are low. Keep an eye on driver updates to squeeze out extra performance over time.

Pros
  • Good 1080p performance
  • Low power draw
  • Affordable
Cons
  • Older architecture
  • Limited VRAM for newer titles
  • Potential wear from mining
Watch out

Make sure the seller confirms the card wasn’t used for prolonged cryptocurrency mining, which can shorten its lifespan.

FAQ

Is 4 GB of VRAM enough for current games?

It handles most 1080p titles fine, but very texture‑heavy games may require turning down settings.

Do I need a special power connector?

The RX 570 typically uses a single 8‑pin connector; ensure your PSU provides it.

What driver version should I install?

Use the latest stable AMD driver from the official website for the best compatibility.
