Sneaky Savings: 1080p Gaming with a $150 Used RTX 2060

A $150 RTX 2060 can still push 1080p titles with modest settings, stretching your budget further.

Takeaway

A used RTX 2060 gives decent 1080p performance without blowing your wallet.

What happened

We sourced a lightly used RTX 2060 for $150 from a local marketplace. In a test build it handled recent AAA titles at 1080p with medium settings, staying above 50 fps on average. The card ran stable after a quick driver update and a basic cleaning.

Why it matters

Many budget builders assume only older cards can fit a tight budget, missing out on newer architectures that offer better efficiency. The RTX 2060 brings hardware‑accelerated ray tracing and DLSS, features often absent in older GPUs. Knowing this expands affordable build options while keeping future‑proofing in mind.

How to think about it

Treat a used RTX 2060 as a stepping stone: pair it with a modest CPU and 8 GB RAM to avoid bottlenecks. Verify the card’s thermal paste and fan condition before purchase. Plan for a power supply that comfortably exceeds the card’s draw to ensure stability.

Pros
  • Solid 1080p performance
  • Ray tracing at low settings
  • Good resale value
Cons
  • Higher power draw
  • Potential fan wear
  • Limited overclock headroom
Watch out

Check for coil whine and verify the card runs cool before buying.

FAQ

Can I run DLSS on a used RTX 2060?

Yes, DLSS works on any RTX card, and it can boost frame rates at 1080p.

Do I need a new PSU for this card?

A quality 450 W PSU is usually sufficient if the rest of the system is modest.

Is the RTX 2060 still supported with driver updates?

NVIDIA continues to provide driver updates for the RTX 2060, keeping it compatible with new games.
