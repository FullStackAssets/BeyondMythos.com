Sneaky Savings: 1080p Gaming with a $115 Used Radeon RX 570

We put a $115 Radeon RX 570 to the test to see if it can still deliver smooth 1080p gameplay on a tight budget.

Takeaway

A $115 RX 570 still handles most modern titles at 1080p with decent settings, making it a solid budget buy.

What happened

We sourced a used RX 570 from a local marketplace, installed it in a modest build, and ran a selection of popular games at 1080p. Performance stayed above 45 FPS on medium settings across the board.

Why it matters

Many builders think older GPUs are dead ends, but the RX 570 shows that a few years old cards can still provide playable experiences without breaking the bank. This extends the life of existing hardware and reduces e‑waste.

How to think about it

When hunting used GPUs, prioritize cards with solid driver support and decent VRAM, and test for any artifacting before committing. Pair the RX 570 with a capable CPU to avoid bottlenecks, and be ready to tweak settings for smoother frames.

Pros
  • Strong 1080p performance
  • Good driver support
  • Affordable on the secondhand market
Cons
  • Higher power draw than newer entry‑level cards
  • Potential fan noise
  • Limited VRAM for future titles
Watch out

Check the card’s thermal paste and fan condition; a neglected cooler can cause overheating under load.

FAQ

Is the RX 570 still supported by AMD drivers?

Yes, AMD continues to release driver updates for the RX 570, ensuring compatibility with newer games.

What power supply is needed for this card?

A reliable 450W PSU with a 6‑pin PCIe connector is generally sufficient.

Can I use the RX 570 for light content creation?

It handles basic video editing and Photoshop tasks, but intensive rendering will be limited.
