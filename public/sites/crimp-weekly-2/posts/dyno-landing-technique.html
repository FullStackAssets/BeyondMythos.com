Dyno Landing Technique

Master the art of landing soft on overhanging problems.

Takeaway

A controlled landing protects your body and keeps momentum for the next move.

What happened

A group of climbers practiced a low‑ball dyno, focusing on bending the knees on impact. They noticed smoother transitions to the next hold and fewer bruises. The technique spread through the gym as a quick fix for hard throws.

Why it matters

Dynos can be risky if the landing is hard, leading to bruises or joint strain. Learning to absorb impact preserves energy for subsequent moves. It also builds confidence to attempt dynamic moves more often.

How to think about it

Approach the dyno with a light step, then explode upward while keeping the core tight. As you land, bend the knees and roll onto the balls of the feet. Keep your hips low to stay balanced for the next move.

Pros
  • Reduces impact
  • Improves flow
  • Builds confidence
Cons
  • Requires timing
  • May feel awkward at first
  • Needs space to practice
Watch out

Avoid landing on a steep slab where a soft roll is impossible.

FAQ

Do I need a crash pad for indoor dynos?

A crash pad is optional but can add safety for higher throws.

Should I lock out my arms on landing?

Keep a slight bend to let the legs absorb most of the force.

Can this technique help on low boulders?

Yes, soft landings benefit any dynamic move regardless of height.
