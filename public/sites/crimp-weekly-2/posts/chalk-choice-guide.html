Chalk Choice Guide

Choosing the right chalk can keep your grip reliable.

Takeaway

Match chalk type to humidity and personal preference for consistent performance.

What happened

Climbers compared loose powder, block, and liquid chalk during a humid session. The powder clumped, the block stayed dry, and the liquid left a thin film that lasted longer. They rotated their chalk based on the day's conditions.

Why it matters

A dry grip prevents slips on small edges and reduces the need for re‑gripping. Using the wrong chalk can make holds feel greasy or cause excessive dust. Consistent grip translates to smoother movement and less frustration.

How to think about it

In dry climates, loose powder works well and is easy to reapply. In humid gyms, a chalk block or liquid version resists moisture better. Carry a small refill of your primary chalk to adjust on the fly.

Pros
  • Improves grip
  • Reduces slip
  • Customizable to conditions
Cons
  • Powder can be messy
  • Blocks add bulk
  • Liquids may feel sticky
Watch out

Avoid over‑applying liquid chalk, as it can make holds feel overly slick.

FAQ

Is liquid chalk allowed in all gyms?

Most gyms allow it, but check local policies before use.

How often should I replace my chalk block?

When it feels hard or no longer leaves a dry film.

Can I mix chalk types?

Yes, many climbers keep a small amount of each for different situations.
