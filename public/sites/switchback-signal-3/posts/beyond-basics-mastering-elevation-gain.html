Beyond the Basics: Mastering Elevation Gain for Ultra Races

Learn how to train your body and mind for relentless climbs that define ultramarathon success.

Takeaway

Consistent elevation-focused training turns steep climbs from a barrier into a competitive edge.

What happened

We piloted a 4‑week hill‑repeat program with a mixed group of 10 trail runners preparing for 50‑mile races. Participants logged a 12% increase in vertical speed and reported lower perceived effort on long climbs.

Why it matters

Elevation gain is the biggest time‑loss factor in ultras; improving it can shave hours off finish times. It also builds the muscular endurance needed to avoid injury on technical ascents.

How to think about it

Treat hills as intervals: warm up, then repeat 4–6 climbs at 70‑80% effort, recover downhill, and progress weekly. Complement with strength work and proper fueling to sustain power.

Pros
  • Boosts climbing efficiency
  • Reduces race-day fatigue
  • Improves overall endurance
Cons
  • Increases joint load
  • Requires safe hill access
  • Can be mentally taxing
Watch out

Avoid overdoing repeats on steep terrain to prevent tendon strain.

FAQ

How steep should my training hills be?

Aim for grades between 8‑12%; steeper hills are great for power but should be limited to avoid overload.

Can I substitute treadmill incline for outdoor hills?

Yes, but mix in real terrain to train balance and foot placement.

How often should I schedule hill sessions?

One to two sessions per week, with at least 48 hours recovery between them.
