Midweek mileage boost: adding 5k without burning out

Add a strategic 5k midweek run to lift your weekly volume while staying fresh.

Takeaway

A short, well‑timed midweek run can raise mileage safely and improve endurance.

What happened

We trialed a 5‑kilometre tempo run on Wednesday for a group of 10 ultrarunners prepping for a 100‑mile race. The session was slotted between a hard interval Saturday and a long Sunday run.

Why it matters

Midweek runs often get skipped, leaving a gap in stimulus that can stall aerobic gains. By filling that gap, runners boost their base without overloading any single day.

How to think about it

Schedule a moderate‑pace 5k at least 48 hours after your hardest workout, keep the effort comfortably hard, and recover with easy strides or cross‑training the next day.

Pros
  • Increases weekly mileage
  • Improves aerobic density
  • Keeps legs loose for long runs
Cons
  • Adds extra time commitment
  • Risk of overreaching if intensity too high
  • May interfere with recovery if not timed right
Watch out

Don’t push the 5k into a hard interval; keep it at a steady, sub‑threshold pace.

FAQ

How fast should the 5k be?

Aim for a pace about 10–15% slower than your threshold – you should be able to hold a conversation in short bursts.

Can I do this on a rest day?

If you truly need a rest day, skip it; the point is to add a low‑impact run, not replace recovery.

What if I’m injured?

Scale back to a brisk walk or cross‑train; the goal is volume, not pain.
