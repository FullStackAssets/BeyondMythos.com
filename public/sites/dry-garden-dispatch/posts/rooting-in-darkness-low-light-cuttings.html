Rooting in Darkness: Does Low Light Boost Cuttings?

We tested succulent cuttings under dim LED light to see if darkness speeds root formation.

Takeaway

A modest shade can nudge roots, but too little light stalls photosynthesis.

What happened

We took 30 leaf cuttings of Echeveria, placed half under 5 lux LED and half under 200 lux. After three weeks we measured root length and health.

Why it matters

Most growers assume bright light is always best, yet cuttings lack chlorophyll initially. Understanding the light balance can cut propagation time.

How to think about it

Start cuttings in low‑light (5‑10 lux) for the first week, then gradually raise to normal propagation light (100‑150 lux). Keep humidity high and avoid direct sun.

Pros
  • Faster root emergence
  • Reduced etiolation risk
  • Easy to implement
Cons
  • Requires controlled lighting
  • May delay leaf greening
  • Not suitable for all species
Watch out

Avoid complete darkness; seedlings still need minimal light to develop chlorophyll.

FAQ

Can I use a dark closet for rooting?

A closet works if you add a dim LED; total darkness stalls growth.

What lux level is considered low light for cuttings?

Around 5‑10 lux mimics a shaded window sill.

Do I need to change the soil mix for low‑light rooting?

Stick with a well‑draining mix; light level doesn’t affect substrate.
