Shortcut Bridge Ride: Cutting Commute Time

Discover a low‑traffic bridge that shaves minutes off your daily loop.

Takeaway

Using quieter bridges can make your ride smoother and quicker.

What happened

I tried the Riverside Pedestrian Bridge on a Tuesday, noting fewer cars and smoother flow. The detour added only a few extra metres but cut the stop‑and‑go at the main intersection.

Why it matters

Less congestion means lower stress and better predictability for your arrival time. It also reduces exposure to busy traffic hotspots.

How to think about it

Map your regular route, then look for nearby bridges or underpasses that parallel busy streets. Test them at off‑peak times and note any changes in travel time or comfort.

Pros
  • Quieter streets
  • Reduced stop‑go
  • Scenic view
Cons
  • Potential extra distance
  • Unfamiliar path
  • Possible signage gaps
Watch out

Watch for cyclists' right‑of‑way rules on shared bridges.

FAQ

Is the bridge legal for cyclists?

Most city pedestrian bridges allow bikes unless signs say otherwise.

Do I need to signal when entering?

A simple hand signal helps alert pedestrians of your approach.

What if the bridge is crowded?

Yield to pedestrians and keep a steady, predictable speed.
