One-pot wonder: a 15-minute campsite stew

A quick, hearty stew that packs flavor and nutrition into a single pot for weekend backpackers.

Takeaway

A single pot can deliver balanced nutrition and save weight on any short trek.

What happened

During a weekend trip in the Cascades, I tried a new 15-minute stew using dehydrated veggies, lentils, and a pre-cooked chicken chunk. The result was a hot, filling meal that required only a 2-liter pot and a small canister stove.

Why it matters

Backpackers constantly juggle weight, time, and flavor; a one-pot solution cuts down on gear and cleanup while still delivering calories. Demonstrating that tasty meals don’t have to be complex encourages more people to cook on the trail.

How to think about it

Start with a base of quick-cooking grains or legumes, add freeze-dried veggies, and finish with a protein packet. Use the stove’s simmer setting to avoid boil-off and stir occasionally to prevent sticking. Pack ingredients in resealable bags for easy portioning.

Pros
  • Lightweight
  • Fast prep
  • Minimal cleanup
Cons
  • Limited variety
  • Requires careful timing
  • May need extra fuel for simmer
Watch out

Watch the pot for scorching if you let the stew boil too hard.

FAQ

Can I substitute beans for lentils?

Yes, just pre-soak or use canned beans to keep cooking time short.

What’s the best stove setting for this stew?

Use a low simmer; a full boil can over-reduce the liquid.

How do I keep the pot from sticking?

Stir frequently and add a splash of oil or a pinch of powdered milk for a smoother texture.
