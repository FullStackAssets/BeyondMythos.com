Rain‑Ready Brake Maintenance

Keep your brakes reliable when wet streets turn slick.

Takeaway

Regularly inspect pads and cables to maintain stopping power in rain.

What happened

A commuter slipped on a wet corner after noticing reduced brake response on a rainy Thursday. After a quick pad check, they discovered worn surface that needed replacement.

Why it matters

Brakes that fade in wet conditions increase the risk of collisions, especially at intersections. Maintaining them prevents sudden loss of control.

How to think about it

Inspect pad thickness monthly, clean rotors with a rag, and test lever feel before rides in forecasted rain. Replace any pad that shows uneven wear.

Pros
  • Improved safety
  • Consistent feel
  • Low cost
Cons
  • Time to check
  • Potential parts cost
  • Requires basic tools
Watch out

Never ride with contaminated or glazed pads; they can dramatically reduce friction.

FAQ

How often should I clean my rotors?

A quick wipe after each wet ride helps prevent buildup.

Can I use any bike grease on brake cables?

Use a light, non‑sticky grease designed for cables to avoid slippage.

Is it okay to ride with slightly worn pads?

If the pad is below the manufacturer’s minimum thickness, replace it promptly.
