Emergency Funds 101

Don't get caught off guard

Takeaway

Save 3-6 months' expenses

What happened

Many of us live paycheck to paycheck, without any cushion for unexpected expenses. A car breakdown or medical bill can quickly lead to debt. Having an emergency fund can provide peace of mind. It's essential to prioritize saving for emergencies to avoid going into debt.

Why it matters

Without an emergency fund, you may be forced to take on high-interest debt or dip into retirement savings. This can have long-term consequences for your financial health. Having a cushion can help you stay on track with your financial goals.

How to think about it

Start by setting aside a small amount each month, and gradually increase the amount over time. Consider setting up automatic transfers from your checking account to your savings or investment account.

Pros
  • Reduces stress
  • Provides peace of mind
  • Helps avoid debt
Cons
  • Ties up cash
  • May earn low interest
  • Requires discipline
Watch out

Don't use your emergency fund for non-essential purchases

FAQ

How much should I save?

Aim for 3-6 months' expenses

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

Consider a high-yield savings account or money market fund

Can I use my emergency fund for anything?

No, only use it for essential expenses, like rent/mortgage, utilities, and food
