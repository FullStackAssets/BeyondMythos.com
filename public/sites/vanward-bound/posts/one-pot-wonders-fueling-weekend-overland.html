One-pot wonders: fueling your weekend overland trips

Maximize flavor and simplicity with single-pot meals that fit any van kitchen.

Takeaway

A well-chosen one-pot recipe saves time, space, and cleanup on the road.

What happened

We tested three classic one-pot dishes—chili, ratatouille, and coconut curry—using a standard 12‑quart pot on a portable induction cooktop during a two‑day weekend trip.

Why it matters

Space and cleanup are premium in a van; reducing the number of dishes and cookware frees up room for living essentials and keeps the kitchen tidy.

How to think about it

Pick recipes with overlapping ingredients, use a pot that fits your induction zone, and plan cooking steps to align with your travel schedule.

Pros
  • Minimal cookware
  • Less cleanup
  • Versatile flavors
Cons
  • Limited browning
  • May require larger pot
  • Potential for stronger odors
Watch out

Watch the pot’s heat level to avoid scorching the bottom of thin sauces.

FAQ

Can I use a regular electric hot plate instead of induction?

Yes, as long as the plate matches the pot’s base and you have adequate power supply.

What’s the best pot material for one-pot meals?

Stainless steel with a thick base works well for even heating and easy cleaning.

How do I prevent strong smells from lingering?

Vent the van while cooking and wipe the pot immediately after use.
