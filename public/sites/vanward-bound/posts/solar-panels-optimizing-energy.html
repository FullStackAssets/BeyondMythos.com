Solar panels on the move: optimizing your van's energy harvest

Maximize solar output on the road with simple placement tweaks and smart wiring tricks.

Takeaway

Smart panel placement and wiring can boost your van’s solar harvest without extra cost.

What happened

We swapped a flat‑mounted panel for a tilt‑mount on a weekend trip and logged daily charge rates. The tilt‑mount consistently added a few extra amp‑hours, especially on cloudy mornings. We also re‑routed the charge controller cables to reduce voltage drop.

Why it matters

Every watt counts when you’re off‑grid; a modest boost in solar intake extends your fridge run time and reduces reliance on noisy generators. Better efficiency translates to more freedom and less stress on limited battery reserves.

How to think about it

Mount panels where they can tilt toward the sun, even if it means a small roof bracket. Keep wiring runs short and use appropriately sized gauge wire to cut loss. Pair your panels with a MPPT controller to extract the most power in varying conditions.

Pros
  • Higher daily charge
  • Reduced generator use
  • Improved battery longevity
Cons
  • Slight weight increase
  • More complex mounting
  • Potential roof leaks if not sealed properly
Watch out

Ensure any roof modifications are watertight; a leak can ruin electronics faster than a low solar output.

FAQ

Do I need a new inverter after adding more solar?

Not necessarily; if your existing inverter handles the peak load, focus on solar and battery sizing.

Can I use flexible panels instead of rigid ones?

Flexible panels work but typically deliver less power per area; they’re best for curved surfaces.

How often should I clean the panels?

A quick wipe every few weeks in dusty areas keeps efficiency up; rain usually does the job in wetter climates.
