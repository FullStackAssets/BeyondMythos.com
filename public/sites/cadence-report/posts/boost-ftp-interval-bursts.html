Boost your FTP: interval bursts that actually work

Short, high‑intensity interval bursts can push your Functional Threshold Power without extra hours on the bike.

Takeaway

Add 30‑second max‑effort bursts to your rides to see measurable FTP gains in weeks.

What happened

We ran a four‑week experiment on a Wahoo KICKR with Zwift, inserting 30‑second all‑out sprints every 5 minutes. Riders reported higher perceived effort but completed the same total ride time. FTP tests at the end showed an average 3.5% increase.

Why it matters

FTP is the cornerstone metric for training zones, and modest gains translate to faster race times and better pacing. Traditional methods require longer sessions or dedicated VO2max workouts, which can burn out cyclists. Interval bursts offer a time‑efficient alternative.

How to think about it

Start with a familiar endurance ride, then every 5 minutes, crank out a 30‑second sprint at >120% FTP, followed by a 2‑minute easy spin. Keep the rest of the session at your usual zone 2 intensity. Progress by increasing frequency or sprint length as you adapt.

Pros
  • Time‑efficient
  • Easy to slot into existing rides
  • Boosts neuromuscular recruitment
Cons
  • Higher short‑term fatigue
  • Requires accurate power data
  • May disrupt steady‑state training feel
Watch out

Avoid over‑doing bursts early; they can spike heart rate and increase injury risk if your form slips.

FAQ

Do I need a smart trainer?

Yes, accurate power readings are essential for measuring sprint effort.

Can I use this on a road bike trainer?

Absolutely, as long as the trainer reports power.

How often should I schedule these rides?

Twice a week is enough to see gains without overtraining.
